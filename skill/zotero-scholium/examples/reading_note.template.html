Full title of the paper

Two or three sentences: authors, venue, what the paper does, and the reason for reading it. Include the code link if one exists.

Method

The method described in paragraphs rather than bullet points. Name the components that carry the contribution and state which of them are new and which are standard. Keep technical terms in the original language.

Results

The figures that matter, together with the comparison they come from (for example, "on 19 datasets the one-step error decreases by about sixty percent against three baselines given the same fine-tuning budget"). Note the cases in which the method performs worse and why that is plausible.

Most valuable part

One or two paragraphs on the experiment or idea that changed the reader's view of the problem, and what would need to be checked before relying on it.

Open questions

Limitations acknowledged by the authors, together with the reader's own observations: unbalanced comparisons, missing baselines, points the text does not explain, cost of reproduction. Unresolved questions may be stated as such.

Reusable elements

Concrete techniques or checks applicable to the reader's own work, with a pointer to the relevant section or table.

Reference

Authors (Year). Title. Venue. DOI / arXiv / URL.
